What Promptcraft actually does

Promptcraft handles the parts around the pictures: the planning, the checking and the packaging. Here's the workflow:

  1. Read a listing. You upload one image of an Etsy listing you admire. Promptcraft works out the product type, who it's for, three versions you could make, and three related ideas to branch into.
  2. Plan every page. It plans your own version page by page, with every word written in: the story text, the exercises and their answers, the planner labels. For a storybook, it creates a cast of characters described the same way on every page, so they look consistent.
  3. Get a prompt for each page. You copy each prompt into your own ChatGPT (or Midjourney or Stable Diffusion) and generate the pictures there.
  4. Proofread. When the pictures come back, Proofread reads the lettering on every page against the plan and flags any page where the AI misspelled something. You redo one page instead of the whole book.
  5. Build the pack. One build gives you:
    • Every page at 300 DPI, in the print sizes you choose
    • A PDF at the exact KDP trim size you pick
    • A commercial licence
    • How-to-print notes
    • Your Etsy title, 13 tags and description
    • Your prompts as a second product you can sell (a prompt pack with its own cover and listing)

On copying: Promptcraft uses the reference listing only for the type of product, its style and its layout. The original's subject, characters and lettering are kept out of your plan, and every page is new artwork you make yourself.

Why "no credits" matters

A lot of AI product tools charge per image or make you buy credit top-ups. Promptcraft doesn't. There are no credits and no API key. The pictures come from the ChatGPT you already pay for.

It also never logs into your ChatGPT or automates it. You paste each prompt yourself, so your ChatGPT account isn't put at risk.

The trade-off: you still do the image-making step yourself, one page at a time.
